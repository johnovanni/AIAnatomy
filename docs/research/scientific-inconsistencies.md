# Scientific inconsistencies: a running list

Places where the app's models and labs, its lesson texts, or the published record disagree with each other or with
what one would expect. Each one is fixed, disclosed in the app, annotated here or in `docs/decisions.md`, or still
open. Several are worth a closer look in a later stage.

Status key:
- **Fixed**: the lab or text now agrees with the record.
- **Disclosed**: the lab still differs, and the app says so plainly on screen or in the lesson.
- **Annotated**: recorded here and in `docs/decisions.md`, with the reason.
- **Open**: not yet resolved.

Decision numbers (D…) refer to `docs/decisions.md`. Positions are counted from 1, as in the app.

| # | Where | Model/lab behaviour | Research/expectation | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attention lab, induction experiment (D16) | The first design (8 random tokens, then the same 8 again) is exactly periodic. A layer-1 head with learned positions learned to look a fixed 7 places back, the loss reached its floor by step 200, and no induction head formed (layer-2 scores stayed under 0.1). | Olsson et al. (2022): in-context copying is done by a previous-token head in an earlier layer composing with an induction head in a later layer, which matches by content. | Fixed and annotated | Sequences now have a random prefix of varying length, a segment of 4 to 8 distinct letters, its repeat and random filler, so only content matching works. Result: layer-2 induction scores 0.82 and 0.90, layer-1 previous-token scores 0.40 and 0.23. The shortcut is told as a teaching point in the Attention lesson. |
| 2 | Position lab and lesson (Note N1, D11) | With position vectors removed, the 2-layer Fables model's predictions still depend on order. The "swap two earlier tokens, nothing changes" invariance holds exactly only for one layer. | A natural expectation is that a transformer without positions is order-blind. Haviv et al. (2022) found causal language models without positional encodings still learn positional information, which they attribute to the causal mask. | Disclosed | The lab's note and the Position lesson say that removing positions takes away the direct signal of order, not every trace of it. |
| 3 | Residual lab (logit lens) and lesson | Early rows of "What would the model guess at each stage?" are often poor guesses. | nostalgebraist (2020) applies the final norm and output layer to intermediate layers; the early-layer readings are rough because those parts were trained only on the last layer's output. | Disclosed | The lab's note and the Residual stream lesson say to read early rows as rough; only the last row is the model's own prediction. |
| 4 | Attention lab, induction training (Note N4) | Our runs show a loss drop between about steps 750 and 1,000 (2.52 to 2.18 in the measured run), when the induction scores rise. | Olsson et al. (2022) report a phase change early in training, "visible as a bump in the training loss", in models with more than one layer, of every size they studied. | Disclosed | Our model and data are far smaller and different (letters, 2 layers, attention only). The lab and lesson call the drop "similar to" theirs, not identical, and not guaranteed at a particular step. |
| 5 | Scale lab and lesson | The app shows GPT-2 small as 124 million parameters. | The GPT-2 paper's Table 2 lists 117M (Radford et al., 2019). OpenAI's release repository says the original counts were wrong and names the small model 124M. | Disclosed | Counting from the published shape (vocabulary 50,257, width 768, context 1,024, 12 blocks, feed-forward 3,072, output tied to the token table, as in the released code) gives 124,439,808, which supports 124M. |
| 6 | Tokens lesson | The lesson gives the range of sub-word vocabulary sizes as "tens of thousands to a few hundred thousand". | The build's guidance (spec 5.2, controller ruling) said "tens of thousands to about 200,000". Gemma's published vocabulary is 256k tokens (Gemma Team, 2024), above that bound. | Annotated | Worded to match the sources checked: GPT-2 50,257 and Gemma about 256,000. Claude's current tokenizer is not public, and the lesson says so. |
| 7 | Architecture map lesson (and spec section 1) | The spec describes Claude as "built on a transformer language model". | No Anthropic publication checked for this task (Claude 3 announcement and model card pages, two research posts, Scaling Monosemanticity) was found to state Claude's architecture outright. Templeton et al. (2024) study the "middle layer residual stream" of Claude 3 Sonnet, which is transformer vocabulary. | Annotated | The lessons say only what was checked: Anthropic's research on Claude 3 Sonnet studies its residual stream, and Claude's exact design, size and training data are not public. The Claude 3 model card PDF was too large to fetch and was not read. Anthropic's 2025 circuit-tracing papers on Claude 3.5 Haiku (Ameisen et al., "Circuit Tracing"; Lindsey et al., "On the Biology of a Large Language Model") reportedly discuss its attention and MLP layers: not yet checked; would justify a slightly stronger sentence. Until then no lesson says "Claude is a transformer". |
| 8 | Embeddings lesson | The lesson invites the reader to look for groups (letters, digits, punctuation, spaces) in the trained picture. | In large word-level models, nearby embeddings often belong to related words (textbook). For a tiny character model, how much grouping survives the 2-D projection was not measured. | Open | The lesson hedges ("look for", "rough map") and points to the percentage of spread kept. Worth measuring: a cluster-separation score for the shipped model's groups. |
| 9 | Embeddings lesson (found in UI rework Task 16) | The shipped Fables model's 69 tokens include no digits: the fables text has none, so the trained picture shows only unknown, space or new line, punctuation and letters. | The lesson says "look instead for groups such as letters, digits, punctuation and spaces". | Fixed | A wording error (Task 16 review ruling). The lesson now says "letters, punctuation, and spaces and new lines (digits too, in a model trained on text that has them)". The lab's legend shows only the groups present. |
